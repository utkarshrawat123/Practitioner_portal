Presentation: a woman in her late thirties, vegetarian, presenting with persistent
bloating after meals, irregular bowel habit, and afternoon energy dips. No red-flag
symptoms; GP had already excluded coeliac disease and confirmed she was not anaemic.

Assessment: dietary review showed a fibre pattern skewed heavily toward raw vegetables
and pulses eaten quickly, low water intake, and meals taken at a desk. The practitioner
framed the picture as functional digestive discomfort in an otherwise well client rather
than a diagnosis.

Approach: the emphasis was on foundational habits first — slower, seated eating,
adequate hydration, and gradually diversifying fibre sources rather than simply
increasing volume. A general food-state multinutrient was considered to cover
background micronutrient adequacy in a vegetarian diet, with the rationale explained to
the client. Nothing was positioned as a treatment for a disease.

Review: at eight weeks the client reported noticeably less post-meal bloating and steadier
afternoon energy. The practitioner noted that the changes were plausibly attributable to
the eating-pattern work as much as to any supplement, and documented that for the record.

Teaching point: foundations before formulas. The most defensible protocols are the ones
where each element has a clear, source-traceable rationale, and where lifestyle change is
given its due weight alongside supplementation.
