Welcome everyone. Today we're looking at how practitioners can support women through
the perimenopausal transition from a food-first, nutrient-informed perspective.

Perimenopause is the years-long transition before periods stop, typically from the
early-to-mid forties. Fluctuating oestrogen and progesterone drive many of the common
presentations practitioners see: disrupted sleep, low mood, changes in energy, and
shifts in how the body handles minerals like magnesium and iron.

Magnesium is one to consider closely. It contributes to normal psychological function,
to normal muscle function, and to a reduction in tiredness and fatigue. Many women in
this life stage report poor sleep quality, and magnesium status is worth reviewing as
part of a wider picture that includes caffeine, alcohol, and evening routine.

Iron deserves attention too, but only where status has been established. Heavier or
more erratic bleeding in perimenopause can deplete iron stores, so a client presenting
with fatigue and low ferritin — confirmed by an appropriate test — may benefit from a
well-tolerated, food-state iron alongside dietary sources. We never load iron blindly.

A note on individualisation: this is about supporting normal physiological function
through a transition, working alongside the client's GP where medication or HRT is
involved. Nothing here replaces medical care, and any client on thyroid medication or
anticoagulants needs an interaction check before supplementing.
